About Dave Haberman

Where This Started

In 2006, while my company was building a seniors housing development in Chemainus on Vancouver Island, I noticed something: a few of our residents were quietly driving their golf carts between home and Mount Brenton Golf Course. Technically, they shouldn't have been — but BC had Low Speed Vehicle legislation on the books, and almost no qualifying cars existed at the time.

That observation planted a seed. On September 24, 2017, my mechanic and I drove the same route in a vintage Dynasty Electric Car — legally, under BC's grandfathered NEV provisions. It was proof that clean, low-speed electric transportation worked. The legislation just needed the vehicles, and the vehicles needed the right environment.

EV Island grew out of that idea: what if you built a community, or a corridor, around low-speed electric vehicles from the start?

The E&N Corridor Connection

The E&N Railway Corridor is 225 kilometres of dormant infrastructure running the length of Vancouver Island. Rail reinstatement keeps being proposed and abandoned. The problem isn't the corridor — it's thinking about it as a rail problem.

Under BC MVA Regulations Section 24.06, it doesn't have to be. It can be an NZEV pathway: low-cost, shovel-ready, and positioned at the intersection of clean energy and reconciliation.

This concept isn't new. Del Webb's Sun City, Arizona opened on January 1, 1960 — the first master-planned community built around low-speed affordable transport. Del Webb graced the cover of TIME Magazine in 1962. Over the following decades, US sunbelt municipalities adopted "Modified Golf Carts" as legal street transportation. That legislation evolved into today's global Neighbourhood EV framework, including BC's NZEV Act.

What I'm proposing for Vancouver Island is the same idea — applied to a 225 km corridor, 14 First Nations territories, and a federal funding window that's open right now.

Background

I'm a retired land developer with experience building seniors housing strata projects, including three projects on Vancouver Island. My home in Chemainus is a custom modular factory build from 2020, featuring solar roof panels, a Level 2 EV charger, and V2H (vehicle-to-home) technology.

In September 2025, our team set up a booth at the Everything Electric Expo in Vancouver — Canada's largest public clean energy event. We ran a rail corridor information campaign and came home with a direct connection to the office of Minister Adrian Dix, Minister of Energy & Climate Solutions.

I recently drove a 2025 BYD Shark up from Mexico to demonstrate to the Island Corridor Foundation board the quality and advanced EV technology coming from China. Transport Canada has granted me a Temporary Import Permit through October 31, 2026. There are only a handful of these trucks in the country.
