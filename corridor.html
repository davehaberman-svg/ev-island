E&N Railway Corridor

225 Kilometres of Opportunity

From Victoria to Courtenay — a dormant rail corridor with a new purpose.

The Problem with Rail Reinstatement

The E&N Railway Corridor has sat unresolved for years. The tracks stretch 225 kilometres from Victoria north to Courtenay, passing through some of Vancouver Island's most significant communities and First Nations territories.

Rail reinstatement is prohibitively expensive leaving the corridor in limbo since passenger service was suspended in 2011.

The NZEV Solution

Under BC Motor Vehicle Act Regulations Section 24.06, the E&N corridor doesn't need to be a rail problem. It can become a Neighbourhood Zero Emission Vehicle pathway: low-speed, low-cost, and purpose-built for the electric future.

A two-way NZEV pathway doesn't require highway-grade construction. Gravel, concrete, asphalt, or wood are all viable surfaces. The corridor's existing right-of-way provides the route — what's needed is the will to convert it.

What Gets Built

The primary benefit of conversion isn't the pathway itself — it's what the pathway enables. A new two-way low-speed corridor opens previously inaccessible land to development. A services corridor built alongside the pathway could generate "a billion dollars in development" along its length.

Potential development along the corridor includes:

  • First Nations member housing utilizing solar generation, V2H charging, and clean energy technologies
  • Cultural and community facilities: longhouses, gathering spaces, cultural programming centres
  • Small commercial spaces: reserve stores, markets, artisan spaces, eco-tourism
  • NZEV education and clean energy demonstration centres
  • Green infrastructure: constructed wetlands, riparian planting, carbon credit projects

Corridor at a Glance

Route: Victoria to Courtenay, Vancouver Island, BC
Length: 225 kilometres
First Nations Territories: 14
Governing Regulation: BC MVA s.24.06
Vehicle Speed: Up to 40 km/h
Surface Options: Gravel, concrete, asphalt, wood

⚖️

Legal Framework

On reserve land in unorganized areas, an NZEV road use permit from the BC Minister of Transportation replaces the municipal bylaw requirement — making First Nations-led pilots directly achievable.

Extensions into adjacent Electoral Area lands require a Joint Use Agreement & Restrictions between the Nation, the BC Ministry of Transportation, and neighbouring corridor landowners.

🌱

Green IPO Potential

Repurposing the defunct E&N corridor at this scale has the elements of a "Green IPO" — a government-supported investment vehicle available to BC residents, unlocking billions in developable land for generations. The First Nations Major Projects Coalition is a natural partner for this dimension.

Stakeholders

Who's at the Table

🏛️

Island Corridor Foundation

The ICF holds corridor-wide stewardship. Nations exercising reversion rights can retain ICF membership — preserving their seat at the table for corridor-wide decisions and eligibility for provincial and federal planning grants.

🏘️

District of North Cowichan

The District has provided significant case study material for developing this program. A continued partnership with the Federation of Canadian Municipalities would help adapt the framework to municipal corridor lands.

🏢

Federation of Canadian Municipalities

The FCM is a key partner in scaling the NZEV corridor model beyond Vancouver Island to other Canadian municipalities with dormant rail corridors.

⚡

Community Energy Association

The CEA supports clean energy integration in BC communities — a natural ally for the energy infrastructure dimension of the corridor development program.

Ready to Move from Dialogue to Action
