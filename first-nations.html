Reconciliation & Clean Energy

First Nations Framework

A replicable model for reclaiming, remediating, and redeveloping E&N corridor land — built with First Nations.

The Framework

The E&N Railway Corridor intersects 14 First Nations territories. EV Island's pilot program — developed in collaboration with the Halalt First Nation — is designed to create a framework that any Nation along the route can adapt and replicate.

The goal is to develop a step-by-step model covering four stages:

  • Land Reversion — Establishing or building on legal precedent to return corridor land to First Nations ownership
  • Environmental Remediation — Addressing contamination and infrastructure hazards before any development
  • NZEV Pathway Conversion — Converting the corridor under BC MVA s.24.06 with road use permits from the Minister of Transportation
  • Land Development — Housing, cultural facilities, commercial space, and green infrastructure enabled by the new pathway

The Snaw-Naw-As Precedent

In 2023, the Snaw-Naw-As First Nation won a BC Court of Appeal case returning their portion of the E&N corridor — establishing a landmark legal precedent. The First Nation now governs that land as reserve land under the Indian Act, retaining full authority over use, leasing, and development.

In 2024, the Snaw-Naw-As completed a Phase 1 and Phase 2 Environmental Site Assessment on their segment. The report confirmed that contamination from creosote-treated railway ties requires active remediation — establishing the process that subsequent Nations can follow.

EV Island offers a model for true reconciliation without going through the courts. By working directly with First Nations, we're transforming the corridor into a zero-emission pathway that benefits everyone — and the money saved from avoiding court battles can power this vision forward.

ICF Membership

Nations exercising reversion rights remain members of the Island Corridor Foundation unless they choose to withdraw. Retaining membership is recommended: it preserves a seat at the table for corridor-wide decisions, maintains eligibility for provincial and federal corridor planning grants, and ensures the Nation's interests are represented in future non-reserve segments.

Halalt First Nation Pilot

EV Island is in active conversation with Chief James Thomas about establishing the pilot on Halalt's 1.53 km corridor segment.

A unique challenge: the raised rail embankment acts as a dyke, trapping Chemainus River floodwater on the northern portion of the reserve. The remediation plan integrates embankment breach and culvert installation — restoring flood drainage and creating opportunities for constructed wetlands and riparian planting.

🌳

Environmental Site Assessment

A Phase 1 and Phase 2 ESA is required before any development or conversion. The Snaw-Naw-As Nation established this process in 2024. Key finding: contamination from creosote-treated ties requires active remediation on most segments.

📜

Road Use Permit

On reserve land in unorganized areas, a road use permit from the BC Minister of Transportation authorizes NZEV operation.

🤝

Joint Use Agreement

Where the NZEV pathway extends beyond reserve boundaries into adjacent Electoral Area lands, a Joint Use Agreement & Restrictions between the Nation, the BC Ministry of Transportation, and neighbouring corridor-owners is required.

What Gets Built

Development Priorities

🏘️

Member Housing

New residential sites utilizing the latest solar generation, V2H (vehicle-to-home) charging, and clean energy technologies. Housing is a top priority for most Nations along the corridor.

🏛️

Cultural Facilities

Longhouses, gathering spaces, and cultural programming facilities — built with the latest clean energy technologies integrated from the ground up.

🛒

Commercial Spaces

Stores, markets, artisan spaces, eco-tourism facilities, and NZEV education and clean energy demonstration centres — enabled by pathway connectivity.

💧

Green Infrastructure

Constructed wetlands, riparian planting, and carbon credit opportunities — especially significant for Nations like Halalt where flood drainage restoration is a core remediation objective.

A Framework Every Nation Can Use

A pilot launched on any one segment creates a replicable framework that every First Nation along the route can adapt. EV Island is committed to supporting that process every step of the way.
