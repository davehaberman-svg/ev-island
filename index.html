The Technology

What is a Neighbourhood EV?

Under BC Motor Vehicle Act Regulations Section 24.06 — which uses the term NZEV — Neighbourhood EVs (NEVs) are four-wheeled electric vehicles capable of speeds up to 40 km/hr. They can operate within defined NEV operating zones with a speed limit of 40 km/hr or less.

These are not golf carts — they are purpose-built electric neighbourhood vehicles, low-cost, low-speed, and built for the electric future Canada is already moving toward.

This EV classification has been legal in BC for decades. My 2001 Kelowna-built NEV (#110) was 20 years ahead of its time.

⚡

NEVs cost a fraction of a full size vehicle to buy and run, making clean transportation affordable and accessible to way more drivers.

The Opportunity

A Shovel-Ready Clean Energy Project

🛤️

289 km Corridor

The dormant E&N railway runs from Victoria to Courtenay passing within 80% of Vancouver Island's immediate population. Reinstating rail service would be prohibitively expensive, highly polluting, at odds with Canada's clean energy goals, and recent studies confirm ridership would be far too low to justify it.

🤝

14 First Nations

The corridor intersects 14 First Nation territories. Returning their land would accelerate reconciliation, let each Nation run the program on their own terms, and end the lawsuits — which would offset the program's costs considerably.

💰

Billions in Development

An NEV corridor would unlock billions in economic development and create thousands of jobs for generations to come.

The Federal Track

Introducing the First Nations Federal NEV Pilot

Vancouver Island and the Gulf Islands are already proving that low-cost, low-speed electric vehicles work — on BC roads, under BC's own Motor Vehicle Act. What's missing isn't demand, community will, or a workable operating framework. It's supply: the affordable, purpose-built Neighbourhood EVs the world is already building aren't yet reaching Canadian roads.

EV Island has applied to the federal government for a Motor Vehicle Safety Act exemption recognizing internationally-proven vehicle certification as an equivalent pathway, alongside the standards Canada and the U.S. already use. It doesn't create a new vehicle category, and it doesn't change a single existing BC rule — it simply opens the door to more affordable, purpose-built vehicles for the Pilot to draw from.

🌐

Two Pilots, Working Together

The Municipal/Provincial NEV Pilot unlocks where Neighbourhood EVs can operate. This Federal Pilot unlocks what can be bought and deployed. Run together, they solve the whole problem — not zoning without supply, or supply without anywhere to drive it.

Where We Start

In 2023, the Snaw-Naw-As First Nation won a BC Court of Appeal case returning their portion of the corridor, establishing a landmark precedent. Their model offers a tested path for other Nations to follow.

  • 2023 Snaw-Naw-As First Nation wins BC Court of Appeal — corridor land returned, landmark precedent set.
  • 2024 Snaw-Naw-As completes Phase 1 & 2 Environmental Site Assessment on their segment. Active remediation of creosote-treated ties required.
  • 2025 EV Island presents at the Everything Electric Expo in Vancouver (Canada's largest public clean energy event), connecting with the office of Minister Adrian Dix, Minister of Energy & Climate Solutions.
  • 2026 Pilot program conversations underway with First Nations.
